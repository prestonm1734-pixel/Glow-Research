Reading it line by line

  • Lot number. The whole point of the document. It ties this report to the specific batch your vial came from, not to the compound in general.
  • Purity by HPLC. The headline number. It measures how much of the material is the target compound versus everything else in the vial.
  • Identity by mass spec. Purity alone cannot tell you the compound is the right one. Mass spec confirms the molecular weight matches what it should be.
  • Endotoxin and water content. Contamination and residual moisture. High water content shortens how long the lyophilized powder stays stable.
  • Lab name and date. A report with no testing laboratory named on it is a marketing document, not a certificate.

Before you order

Questions worth asking any supplier.

Including us. If a supplier cannot answer these, that tells you something.

What to ask for

  • The report for your lot, not a sample report for the compound.
  • The name of the testing laboratory, so the result can be checked independently.
  • Both purity and identity. A purity number on its own does not prove the compound is what the label says.
  • The date of analysis. A report from two years ago says nothing about the batch in your hand.

Where we stand

Every compound is manufactured domestically and independently verified above 99% purity. Identity, purity, endotoxin, and sterility are checked through the batch, not only at the end of it, and each step is logged against the lot number.

Full manufacturing and QC documentation is available on request for qualified researchers. Ask and we will send it.
